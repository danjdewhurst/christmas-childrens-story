## Chapter Text

Pippa knelt in the snow by the warm glow. Her whiskers trembled.

"Quick-quick!" called a voice from the sky. Wrenna Sparrow swooped down with a swish of brown wings. Snow puffed up around her feet.

"Ooh!" said Pippa. "The Star fell down. It went poof, and now it glows."

Wrenna bent to peek close beside her. The Borrowed Star lay in the snow, small as a pinecone and peach-gold all over. It pulsed soft and sleepy.

Just then Bram Tinker-Mole huffed up the hill. He was round and slow, with snow on his nose and a warm dough cloth in his paws. He smelled like fresh bread.

"Slow and steady," said Bram. "I brought something soft. For carrying stars."

Old Tobias Clockkeeper came next, in his long coat, with kind eyes. He carried the jam jar lid with star holes. Pippa had left the jar by the door in her hurry.

"Thank you, little one," said Tobias. "You saved just what we need."

Together they scooped. Bram slid the dough cloth under the Star. Wrenna lifted gentle, gentle with her beak. Pippa held the jam jar wide. Plip! In went the Star, warm and glowing.

Pippa sang a tiny thank-you song. The glow grew still and steady.

Tobias knelt low beside her. "Stars borrow their light," he said. "This one fell, and now it must rest. Before dawn, it must sleep in the Stone Nest atop the Wishing Pine on Starberry Hill. Keep it warm. Hold it in turns. Sing to it."

Pippa lifted the jar all by herself. Oof! It was too heavy. Her arms wobbled.

Bram smiled. "Slow and steady is better with friends," he said.

Pippa unwound her red thread scarf from her neck. They tied it into a sling, so each friend could take a turn.

Then Tobias pressed a wobbly brass button into her paw. "For luck, little one," he said.

Granny Nutmeg the hedgehog bustled up with her basket. The air smelled of cinnamon and honey. "My dears," she said. "You look cold. Take two." She gave each of them a warm courage bun.

Pippa took a nibble. Crunchy top, soft middle. Yum.

Tobias looked at the sleepy blue lamps of Tinsel Hollow. Then he looked east, where morning would come.

"We have till morning light," he said.
