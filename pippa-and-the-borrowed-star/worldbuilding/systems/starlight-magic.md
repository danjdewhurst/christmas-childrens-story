## Overview

Starlight magic is the warm light of the Christmas Star. It lives in lamps, starberries and kind acts. It grows when people share and shrinks when people keep it to themselves. Children and animals feel it first as warmth in their chests.

## Rules & Limitations

Starlight follows four simple rules.

1. Sharing makes it brighter. Giving light, food or comfort adds glow.
2. Hiding makes it dimmer. Hoarding, fibbing or refusing help cools it.
3. Rest keeps it healthy. Lamps need snuffing at dawn and the Star needs dark naps.
4. Thanks seals it in. A spoken thank you holds the glow longer.

Starlight cannot force anyone, cannot melt snow in a rush and cannot work if demanded. It fades fast in loud, greedy hands and returns slow with gentle care.

## History

The first travelers followed starlight down into Tinsel Hollow. They learned to keep lamps lit on Christmas Eve to say thanks. Old Tobias learned winding songs that steady clocks. Granny Nutmeg learned soup recipes that hold warmth well. Each year the wishing pine stores a little extra glow in its bark.

## Practitioners

Anyone can carry starlight. No training is needed, only kindness. Pippa carries it in the jar. Bram mends lanterns so they hold it safely. Wrenna bears messages between hill and village. Old Tobias tends tower light. Granny Nutmeg feeds carriers.

## Impact on Society

Starlight sets village rhythm. Lamps mark bedtime. Soup nights share stored warmth. Wishes on the hill teach children to give thanks. When the Star dims, clocks slow, berries taste thin and folks stay close to home till light returns.
